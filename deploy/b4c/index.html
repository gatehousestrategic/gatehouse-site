The problem

Riders are ranked on one thing: how much money they raised. Some riders know wealthy people and can raise $100,000 with a few phone calls. Others ask hundreds of friends, one by one, and raise $25,000. The first rider is celebrated. The second, who often worked much harder, is barely noticed.

  • Hard work and reach go unrecognized
  • Most riders can never get near the top of the list
  • Donors get a receipt and little else
Proposed

The solution

Keep celebrating the top fundraisers. Add honors for the other kinds of effort: how many people a rider got to give, how much they grew, and how long they have ridden. Every rider has a real way to be recognized, and donors get recognized too.

  • Eight honors instead of one ranking
  • Calculated automatically from the donations
  • Donors get their own history and thanks

How it works

Eight ways to
be recognized

Like the jerseys in a pro bike race, each honor rewards a different strength. Each gets its own leaderboard, a mark on the rider's page, and a moment on stage at the ride.

Top Fundraiser

Most money raised. Stays exactly as it is today.

Community Builder

Most people and companies who gave.

New Faces

Most donors giving to Bike4Chai for the first time.

Climber

Biggest growth over the rider's own total last year.

Streak

Most years ridden in a row.

Keeper

Most of last year's donors giving again.

Rookie

Best first-year rider.

Goal Crusher

Hit their own goal, whatever the size. Every rider who does it earns it.

Donors and teams

Donors and teams
get recognized too

A donor history

Every donor sees what they have given across years and riders. Anyone who gives three years in a row, at any amount, is named at the ride.

A real thank-you

Riders are reminded to send a personal thank-you after each gift. Each gift is shown as what it pays for, like a day of Camp Simcha.

Fairer team rankings

Teams are also ranked by donors per rider and by how many of their riders hit their goal, not only by total dollars.

Keeping it fair

Simple counting
rules

Corporate gifts

Count fully, for dollars and as a donor. A company is one donor per rider, however many times it gives.

Employer matches

Add to dollars. The person and their employer's match count as one donor.

Small gifts

Every dollar counts. A gift has to be $18 or more to count as a donor.

A rider's own gifts

Add to dollars, but don't count as donors.
