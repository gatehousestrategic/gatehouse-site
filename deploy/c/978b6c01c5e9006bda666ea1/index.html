What a general billing company can't give you.

Behavioral health is all we bill. Three things follow from that.

  • Clinicians run utilization review

    Most billing companies treat authorizations as paperwork. Ours are led by licensed clinicians who read documentation the way a payer's reviewer will: medical necessity, continued-stay criteria, level of care. They handle initial authorizations, concurrent reviews and peer-to-peer support, so fewer days are denied and your clinical staff spends less time on the phone with insurers.

  • No offshore team, anywhere in the process

    Billing, collections and UR all happen in the U.S. You deal directly with experienced people who know American payer rules, answer when you call, and work as part of your team rather than behind a ticket queue.

  • Medicare, Medicaid and commercial, each on its own terms

    The three payer types pay differently, document differently and deny for different reasons. We bill all of them every day, and we know where each one tends to cost a program money.

Your EMR is not a billing team.

An EMR can generate a claim. It can't tell you the claim is wrong for that payer, notice an authorization is about to lapse, or chase an underpayment. That takes people who know the rules, a defined workflow and someone checking the work.

Service details

Verification of benefits

Authorizations and UR

Coding and claims

Payment posting

Denials and appeals

Denials worked to a decision, and the cause fixed upstream so the same denial does not come back next month.

A/R recovery

Outstanding balances, including older claims a previous biller left behind, followed until they are paid or closed.

Reporting

A clear view for leadership of what has been paid, what is pending and what is stuck.

Three payer types. Three rulebooks.

Medicare

Coverage requirements, provider eligibility and documentation standards specific to PHP, IOP and opioid treatment services.

Medicaid

State-specific rules, managed care organization requirements, authorization processes and reimbursement structures that vary plan to plan.

Commercial

Payer contracts, medical necessity guidelines, coding policies and authorization requirements that differ by carrier and by plan.

Methadone clinics and Opioid Treatment Programs

OTP and MAT billing has little in common with ordinary outpatient billing. Bundled payments, HCPCS coding, medication administration and counseling services each carry their own rules, and Medicare and Medicaid handle them differently.

We bill for these programs regularly, and we work their denials and underpayments with that model in mind.
