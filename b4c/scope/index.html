How gifts are counted, and why

There are two counts. Dollars measure how much money a rider raised, so every real dollar counts. Donors measure how many people a rider reached, so a gift only adds a donor when it shows someone new chose to back that rider. That's why the two columns don't always match.

Type of giftAdds to dollarsCounts as a donorWhy
Individual gift, $18 or more✓✓Once per riderA real person chose to back this rider. If they give again, it's still one person, so repeat gifts add dollars but not extra donors.
Gift under $18✓✕Every dollar still helps, so it counts toward dollars. It doesn't add a donor, so nobody can pad their count with a pile of $1 gifts. $18, one chai, is the line.
Corporate gift✓✓One company, one donor per riderGetting a business to give takes the same ask as getting a person, so it counts. A company that gives several times is still one supporter.
Employer match✓✕The person and the match count onceThe match is real money for Chai Lifeline, so it counts toward dollars. But it only happened because one person gave, so counting both would count one decision twice.
Anonymous gift✓✓The name is hidden from the public, but Bike4Chai knows who gave, and the rider still earned that gift.
Rider's own or household gift✓✕It's real money, so it counts toward dollars. The donor count is about reaching other people, and a rider giving to themselves or their own household doesn't reach anyone new.
Gift split across riders✓Each rider's share✓For each riderThe money is divided, not copied, so the event total still adds up. The donor chose to back each of those riders, so each one counts them.

07Your call

Keep Neon, or move
to a new system?

The honors can work either way. The real question is what handles your donations: Neon, which does it today, or a new system built for Bike4Chai. Here are the three ways to do it, in plain terms, with what each means for your donors, riders, staff and budget. You can pick one in the questions form at the bottom of this page.

A

Keep Neon, add the honors on top

Donors keep giving through Neon exactly like today. The new site reads the donations from Neon and builds the honors and leaderboards from them.

The good

  • Nothing changes for donors, riders or staff
  • Every way of paying keeps working
  • No risk to the money coming in
  • Quickest to launch

The not so good

  • Your staff work in two systems instead of one
  • Leaderboards may be a few hours or a day behind, depending on what Neon allows
  • Rider and donation pages stay on Neon, so their look can only change a little
  • Splitting one gift across riders and buying sponsorships online may not be possible
  • Neon's fees continue
B

Move to a new system for everything

A new system built for Bike4Chai replaces Neon completely: donations, registration, receipts and rider pages all move over.

The good

  • Everything in one place, designed around Bike4Chai
  • Honors show everywhere, the moment a gift comes in
  • Split gifts, monthly giving and online sponsorships
  • Bike4Chai owns all of its data in one place
  • No Neon platform fee; card processing fees still apply

The not so good

  • Highest upfront cost and the longest build
  • A brand-new system handles your donations in its first season, including ride day
  • Some ways of paying, such as donor account cards, may not all be ready at launch
  • Staff, riders and returning donors learn a new system
  • Seventeen seasons of history have to move over correctly before launch
  • An ongoing support and hosting cost replaces Neon's
C

Start with Neon, decide after 2027

The 2027 ride uses option A. After the season, you decide whether to stay on Neon or move your donations to a new system.

The good

  • Honors launch for 2027
  • No change to how donations work in the first year
  • You decide after seeing a season of the honors
  • Staying on Neon remains an option

The not so good

  • If you switch later, some of the first year's cost is spent twice
  • The new ways of giving wait at least a year
  • Your staff work in two systems for at least one season
  • A second decision, and possibly a second change for donors and riders, a year later
